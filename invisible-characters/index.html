Why invisible characters ruin your day

Copy a line out of Word, a PDF, a Jira ticket or a chat message and you often bring passengers: a non-breaking space that looks exactly like a space, a zero-width space that occupies no width at all, a smart quote where you expected an apostrophe. The text on screen is identical. Every exact-match comparison fails anyway.

The Stack Overflow questions for just two of these — removing U+200B zero-width space and stripping non-printable Unicode in JavaScript — have over 84,000 views between them, and that is one language and two characters out of dozens.

The dangerous ones

Bidirectional overrides (U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069) reorder how text is displayed without changing what it actually says. In source code this is the Trojan Source attack (CVE-2021-42574): a reviewer reads one program and the compiler builds a different one. This tool flags them as critical wherever they appear.

Homoglyphs are letters from another alphabet that look identical to Latin ones — Cyrillic а versus Latin a. They are how lookalike domains and lookalike package names work, and how a variable name silently becomes a second, different variable.
